Use "if I were" for imaginary, hypothetical, or contrary-to-fact situations. Use "if I was" for real or possible situations that actually happened in the past.

If I Were (Subjunctive Mood)

• What it means: You are talking about a dream, a wish, or something that is not true right now.
• Example: "If I were rich, I would buy a house." (You are not rich)
• Example: "If I were you, I would take that job." (You are not that person; common for advice)

If I Was (Indicative Past Tense)

• What it means: You are talking about something that really happened or might have happened in the past.
• Example: "If I was rude yesterday, I am sorry." (You might have actually been rude)
• Example: "If I was late to class, the teacher yelled at me." (A real event from your past)
Note: In casual, informal speech, many native speakers use "if I was" for hypothetical situations, though "if I were" is preferred in formal writing and tests.

if I was to or if I were to?

Use "if I were to" for imaginary or highly unlikely future events, and "if I was to" only for informal or casual conversations.
In formal English, grammar tests, and professional writing, "if I were to" is the only correct choice for hypothetical situations.

The Breakdown

• If I were to (Correct/Formal): This uses the subjunctive mood to show that an action is purely hypothetical, unlikely, or impossible.
	• Example: "If I were to win the lottery, I would travel the world." (Highly unlikely)
	• Example: "What would happen if I were to press this red button?" (Hypothetical scenario)
• If I was to (Informal): This is very common in everyday casual speech among native speakers. While understood, it is technically considered grammatically incorrect in structured or formal writing.
	• Example: "If I was to guess, I'd say he's about thirty." (Fine to say to a friend, but change to were in an essay).

Why do we use "were to"?

Adding "to" plus a verb (e.g., were to go, were to ask) makes a hypothetical situation sound even more distant or unlikely than just using the simple past tense.
• "If I asked him, he would say no." (Standard hypothetical)
• "If I were to ask him, he would say no." (Emphasizes that I am very unlikely to actually ask him)
